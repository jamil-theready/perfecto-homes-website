<p>A new international airport is under construction in Chinchero, Peru. It sits just 15 minutes from Urubamba and under an hour from Ollantaytambo. When it opens, the Sacred Valley will go from a side trip off Cusco to a primary destination with direct international access. For property investors, this changes everything.</p>

<h2><strong>The Chinchero Airport Project</strong></h2>

<p>Peru's government has committed billions of soles to the Chinchero International Airport (IATA code: still pending). The project replaces the aging Alejandro Velasco Astete Airport in Cusco, which cannot accommodate larger aircraft due to its short runway and high altitude location inside the city.</p>

<p>Chinchero sits at approximately 3,700 meters elevation on a wide plateau. The new facility will feature a runway long enough for wide body jets, a modern passenger terminal, and capacity to handle millions of travelers per year. Direct flights from Lima, other South American capitals, and eventually North American and European cities are all part of the long term plan.</p>

<p>Construction has faced delays over the years, but the project has strong political backing and active investment. Current projections target completion within the next few years. Ground work and infrastructure development are well underway.</p>

<h2><strong>Why This Airport Matters for Real Estate</strong></h2>

<p>Right now, reaching the Sacred Valley requires flying into Cusco, then driving 60 to 90 minutes through winding mountain roads. The Chinchero airport eliminates that friction. Travelers will land directly in the Sacred Valley. That single change has massive implications for Chinchero airport real estate and the surrounding towns.</p>

<p>Consider what happened in other markets when airports opened or expanded:</p>

<ul>
<li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Cancun property values surged after its international airport became a major hub.</p></li>
<li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Queenstown, New Zealand saw real estate prices climb steadily as its airport added international routes.</p></li>
<li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Tulum land prices multiplied as the new Felipe Carrillo Puerto airport neared completion.</p></li>
</ul>

<p>The pattern is consistent. Airport access drives tourism volume. Tourism volume drives demand for accommodation, dining, and services. That demand pushes Chinchero airport property values upward.</p>

<h2><strong>Price Projections for the Sacred Valley</strong></h2>

<p>The Sacred Valley is still in an early stage compared to established tourism real estate markets. Land in Urubamba and Ollantaytambo sells for a fraction of what comparable parcels cost in Mexican resort towns or Southeast Asian hotspots.</p>

<p>Industry observers expect property values in the Sacred Valley to increase 30% to 60% in the years following the airport opening. Properties closest to the airport and along the main tourism corridor between Chinchero, Urubamba, and Ollantaytambo will likely see the sharpest gains.</p>

<p>Hospitality properties stand to benefit the most. Hotels, hostals, and restaurants that serve international visitors will see higher occupancy rates and stronger revenue. The math is simple: more flights means more visitors means more demand for beds and tables.</p>
