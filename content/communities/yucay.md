## Yucay: A Royal Inca Retreat in the Sacred Valley

Yucay is a small village tucked between Urubamba and Calca, about 70 kilometers from Cusco. The town's history runs deep — Inca emperor Sayri Tupac built his royal estate here in the 16th century, and the church and colonial buildings around the central plaza still echo that era. For modern buyers, Yucay offers some of the most peaceful and prestigious living in the Sacred Valley.

### A Boutique Hotel Destination

Yucay has long been a favorite of boutique hotel operators. The Sonesta Posadas del Inca and other heritage properties occupy converted colonial buildings around the plaza, drawing guests who want a quieter alternative to the busier Sacred Valley towns. The hospitality infrastructure here is mature and well-regarded.

For investors interested in the lodging space, Yucay offers properties with historical character, established tourist traffic from neighboring sites, and a quality-over-quantity guest base. Wedding venues, retreat centers, and high-end small inns all do well here.

### Real Estate in Yucay

Yucay real estate is limited in volume but high in quality. The village is small, so inventory turns over slowly. When properties come on the market, they tend to be unique — colonial homes, working farms with century-old irrigation systems, or view parcels along the riverbanks.

Land prices reflect the village's prestige and the limited supply, but the calculus for many buyers is straightforward: Yucay offers a quality of place that the larger towns cannot match.

### Agriculture and Pastoral Land

The valley floor around Yucay is intensely cultivated. Corn, the famous giant white Cusco corn, grows in fields that have been farmed continuously for over five centuries. Many properties include working farmland that can either be leased to local farmers or operated by the new owner.

The village sits at about 2,857 meters, with mild temperatures and predictable seasons. The growing climate is among the best in Peru.

### Why Yucay Matters

Yucay is the kind of place that does not appear on most tourist itineraries, which is exactly why it appeals to buyers who have spent time in the valley. The pace is slow, the neighbors are mostly long-term residents, and the surrounding land has been farmed by the same families for generations. It is the Sacred Valley distilled.

The town benefits from proximity to Urubamba's services (10 minutes by car) without inheriting any of its bustle.
